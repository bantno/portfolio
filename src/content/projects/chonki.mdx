Combat robotics pits custom-built machines against each other in an enclosed arena — Chonki competes in the featherweight class (30 lb, or 45 lb with the shuffler weight bonus) at NHRL, the premier US combat robotics league.

## Approach

Designed and fabricated a 45 lb featherweight combat robot across multiple competition seasons, with each season driving a targeted redesign:

- **Weapon.** Geometry changes to improve bite and energy storage.
- **Armor.** Material and thickness tradeoffs to survive increasingly powerful opponents.
- **Drivetrain.** Gearing adjustments to balance speed against pushing power.

Alongside the engineering, held five leadership roles over six years in Georgia Tech's RoboJackets: Mechanical Training Lead and 3 lb Mentor (2019–2020), Sponsorship Chair and Team Lead (2020–2021), and Team Mentor (2021–Present).

## Iteration

Chonki started as a name, not a design. The goal: exploit the 1.5× weight bonus NHRL grants robots using non-wheeled locomotion, and spend that extra mass proving shell spinners could be competitive in the featherweight class.

### Chonki (2020)

Designed during the COVID-19 pandemic but never built — manufacturing restrictions made fabrication impossible. It existed only in CAD, but established the shell spinner + shuffler drivetrain architecture every later version builds on.

### Chonkii (2021–2022)

The first physical Chonki — big, loud, and impossible to ignore; the shufflers scraping steel arena floors required hearing protection at competition. Chonkii proved the concept viable, beating Marathon (then ranked #3) at Motorama, but left significant room to improve.

### Chonkiii (2022–2023)

A major design leap, and the first Chonki to compete at NHRL. It introduced a hardened 4140 alloy steel shell — proving the CNC billet approach that defines every later version — plus a sloped wall shell, reduced ground clearance, a self-righter, and dual weapon drive motors.

Three problems remained:

- **Two-piece impactors** were prone to bolt failures.
- **2:1 drive gearing** was underpowered.
- **Shuffler modules**, while reliable, lacked the torque to drive effectively against strong opponents.

### Chonkiv (2023–2025)

A systematic redesign targeting every Chonkiii shortcoming:

- **Teeth:** Single-piece sharpened AR600 toothicanes replaced the failure-prone two-piece AR500 impactors, with a larger rake angle for better bite depth and cutting efficiency.
- **Drive:** 3:1 bevel gear reduction (up from 2:1) delivered substantially more pushing power — enough to push opponents without the shell spinning.
- **Shuffler pods:** Modular, self-contained units, field-swappable in ~15 minutes for rapid pit repairs.
- **Electronics:** 3D-printed enclosures with dedicated battery slots and wire-routing channels replaced loose wiring.
- **Frame:** Puzzle-fit 7075 aluminum with M4 fasteners (up from M3), waterjet-cut to tight tolerances for a rigid chassis with minimal fastener reliance.

<Figure src="/images/chonki/chonkiv_both_copies_2025-worlds.JPG" alt="Both Chonkiv copies at 2025 NHRL World Championship" caption="Both Chonkiv copies at 2025 NHRL World Championship" number={4} />

**The result:** a 16–3 record with 12 knockouts across 19 fights, two Golden Dumpster trophies, and a rise to #1 in the world — the most successful shell spinner campaign in modern featherweight competition.
