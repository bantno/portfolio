## The Challenge

Autonomously capture rogue drones in indoor, GPS-denied, densely populated environments — a scenario where traditional GPS-based navigation fails and obstacle avoidance must happen in real time.

## Approach

- **Control stack.** A ROS2 architecture splitting a Raspberry Pi companion computer (high-level planning and state logic) from a Pixhawk running PX4 (low-level flight control), bridged with micro-XRCE-DDS so ROS2 nodes command PX4 directly through its offboard interface.
- **Indoor positioning.** A Vicon motion-capture feed fused into PX4's EKF2 estimator replaces GPS entirely.
- **Target tracking.** The interceptor treats its target as a second vehicle, subscribing to the target's local position over a separate PX4 namespace, and plans a trajectory to intercept and capture it from above.
- **Payload.** A 3D-printed (PLA, NylonX) mechanical grasper with spring-loaded jaws and a servo-actuated release, capable of securing a target drone in flight.

## Path Planning

The production planner wraps OMPL's **RRT\***: it samples the configuration space at random, connects each sample to its nearest node, and rewires the tree whenever a shorter path appears.

- **Workspace:** a bounded 3D volume of ±4 m per axis, against spherical obstacle constraints.
- **Search parameters:** goal-sampling bias of 0.1 and a 1.0 m extension step.
- **Real-time budget:** each solve runs under a fixed ~0.45 s budget, so the tree can be regrown as the target moves.
- **Approach constraint:** a path-validity check enforces that the final approach comes from above the target.
- **Smoothing:** resulting waypoints get a Bézier or B-spline fit before being handed to PX4.

## Grasping Mechanism

The grasper uses spring-loaded jaws that close on contact, with a servo-actuated release for controlled deployment. NylonX construction provides impact resistance while keeping weight under the drone's payload budget.

## Iteration

**Trajectory stack.** Started as a single closed-form quadratic Bézier curve — cheap and smooth, but blind to obstacles and only viable in open space. Moving to OMPL RRT\* added true obstacle avoidance and the approach-from-above constraint needed for a reliable capture, while the fixed per-solve time budget keeps planning fast enough to re-plan against a moving target. The Bézier/B-spline post-smoothing carried over to keep the executed path flyable.

**Grasping mechanism.** Three revisions:

- **V1** — rigid jaws transferred impact loads directly to the drone frame, causing attitude disturbances.
- **V2** — added a compliant wrist joint that absorbed impact energy.
- **V3** (final) — combined the compliant wrist with spring-loaded jaws, eliminating the actuator needed to close the gripper and **reducing weight by 40%**.
