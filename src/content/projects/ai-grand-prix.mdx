## The Competition

The [AI Grand Prix](https://www.theaigrandprix.com/) is an autonomous drone racing competition conceived by Anduril founder Palmer Luckey, with the Drone Champions League and Neros Technologies — a **$500,000** prize pool and engineering roles at Anduril. Teams qualify in simulation, then fly the same autonomy on a real drone.

## Phase One — The Simulator Qualifier

The organisers disable GPS, attitude, position — **and the gate coordinates.** Everything the drone knows about the course, it has to see and solve for in flight.

**Result: 7th of 3,000+ teams.** Best lap 17.56 s, all 17 gates, zero collisions, pure vision.

<Details summary="Gate detection" hint="the only source of position">

The only source of gate localization — each camera frame becomes range, bearing, and where resolvable a full 6-DOF gate pose, each tagged with explicit trust flags telling the estimator which parts to believe.

- **Segmentation is brightness-invariant.** Gate markers pulse, so absolute HSV floors produce an unstable blob frame to frame. The shipped mask keys on **red dominance** rather than absolute brightness, closed morphologically to bridge the overlay artifacts that would otherwise sever a ring into an open arc.
- **Non-gate red is rejected structurally, not by hue.** A real gate is a hollow, near-square ring; the ceiling arrows and the "AI-GP" banner are solid. The test is on shape, so a change in lighting can't turn signage into a gate.
- **Pose recovery cascades by range.** A fully-visible ring solves for full 6-DOF pose against the known gate geometry. As the gate clips the frame, the pipeline degrades deliberately — to the inner opening, then to a geometric construction from whichever free edges remain, then to a bearing-only centroid — with each regime declaring its own trust level.
- **Range is only trusted when it's earned.** Measured blob edges have a hard floor in pixels — glow halo, minimum line width, compression bleed — so past a certain distance every gate back-projects to nearly the same range. Trust is therefore tested on *measured pixel size*, never on the derived range, under a single continuous law that inflates range uncertainty as the edge approaches that floor and diverges at it.
- **Bearing has no such floor**, so a range-floored far gate still contributes an accurate bearing. That asymmetry is what lets the near gate anchor the along-track axis while far gates resect heading — and the handover happens automatically as gates approach.
- **Classical beat learned.** Zero-shot ML detectors produced no usable detections against the simulator's stylized aesthetic; a classical pipeline is the shipped backend.
- **Bounded compute.** Blobs are processed largest-first under a fixed wall-clock budget, so a many-gate frame can't starve the receive and control threads. Truncation only ever drops the far tail.

</Details>

<Details summary="State estimation" hint="15-state error-state Kalman filter">

A 15-state error-state Kalman filter in the Solà (2017) formulation — nominal state `[p, v, q, a_b, ω_b]`, error state `[δp, δv, δθ, δa_b, δω_b]` with a body-frame attitude error.

- **The accelerometer is an input, not a measurement.** There is deliberately no thrust model, no hand-scheduled trust modulation, and no hand-gained residual feedback anywhere in the filter. The only state that can absorb a specific-force error is the accel bias, and it moves solely through a covariance-weighted Kalman gain bounded by its own process noise.
- **Exact Jacobians.** The covariance transition matrix is the true first-order Jacobian of the propagator — including the right-Jacobian factor the textbook form omits — proven against a numerical Jacobian in the test suite.
- **Four measurement types**, each with a closed-form Jacobian and χ²-gated innovation: body-frame landmark position against the gate map, gate-plane normal as the attitude anchor, a **rotor-drag** pseudo-measurement (Faessler 2018) that makes velocity observable with no vision at all, and a yaw pseudo-measurement. A pre-arm at-rest routine (ZUPT, gravity levelling, gyro bias) initializes the filter on the launch ramp.
- **Reject, never force-fit.** Corrections use the Joseph form with Solà's post-injection reset. Gating is two-tier: beyond the 99.9% χ² quantile is a hard reject, while 95–99.9% inflates the measurement noise so a marginal fix is admitted only up to its validated magnitude. The state correction is never clamped — that would desynchronize the covariance from the state.
- **Camera latency is handled by rewinding, not approximating.** Vision fixes are stamped at frame capture and arrive after the filter has integrated past that epoch. A ring of state snapshots lets the filter restore the nearest snapshot, apply the update *there*, and re-propagate the buffered IMU forward (the Lynen 2013 / `ethzasl_msf` pattern) — exact, at a cost of a few propagate steps.
- **Noise is measured, not tuned.** Each sighting's covariance is built from calibrated residual laws on the axes the detector actually errs along — including an attitude-rate-coupled centre wander treated as a *colored* process so a white-noise filter can't double-count it, and an independent misorientation witness: a bolted-down gate cannot physically rotate between frames, so the angle between the solved and mapped normal measures this frame's pose error directly.

</Details>

<Details summary="Control" hint="cascaded SE(3) geometric tracking">

A cascaded SE(3) geometric tracking controller in the Lee et al. (2010) lineage, flying through gate openings 1.5 m square.

- **Reference.** An arc-length-reparameterised spline through the gate opening centres is the sole reference path.
- **Speed governor.** A progress variable marches along the spline at the most restrictive of a flat speed cap, a curvature-limited cornering cap, and a descent cap — previewed ahead of the vehicle and rate-limited so the reference can't step.
- **Cascade.** Translational PD produces a world acceleration command, decomposed yaw-aware into roll/pitch setpoints under a tilt clamp; an attitude-P loop with feed-forward emits body rates.
- **Plant inversion.** Body rates are pushed through the exact inverse of the *measured* plant — a nonlinear rate gain, mount rotation, and sign chain mirrored precisely against the estimator's sensor mapping so sensing and command remain true inverses. Throttle is tilt-compensated through a calibrated thrust map.

</Details>

<Details summary="Verification and results" hint="scored against an independent observer">

Flight accuracy is scored **only** against an independent ground-truth observer running as a separate read-only process — never against the estimator's own state.

- **Offline CI gate.** Two test suites plus roughly 60 standalone tests, covering numeric-vs-analytic Jacobians, covariance symmetry and positive-definiteness, reset behaviour, reduction to known cases, and NIS/NEES consistency on synthetic data.
- **Per-run consistency reporting.** Channel-by-channel NIS statistics accumulate every run — mean ≈ degrees of freedom, ~5% beyond the 95% gate for a consistent filter.
- **Batch A/B harness.** Arm-tagged batches run from spec files with venue-health tripwires; findings accumulate as a version-controlled experiment corpus alongside the code.

The counted record of **17.56 s** came from successive tuning of the speed governor, corner authority and per-gate aim; the first full-course completion on pure vision — all 17 gates, zero collisions — was **22 July 2026**, and estimator accuracy held at **~0.8 m position RMS** against ground truth over 253 m of flight.

</Details>

## Phase Two — The Physical Qualifier

The same stack is being brought up on real drones, one subsystem at a time.

<Video src="/images/ai-grand-prix/onboard_gate_detection.mp4" caption="The gate detector running on onboard video from the qualifier venue." />

Autonomous hover and multi-gate course legs are flying today. The full course is not yet flown autonomously — the qualifier is live, so the sections below give the architecture but not the tuning, and what remains stays with the team.

<Details summary="What flies today" hint="as of 19 September 2026">

These are bring-up milestones on a live campaign, not a finished racer.

- **Fully autonomous hover, three flights for three.** Gollum took off, held its commanded height for 30 s and landed by itself; the pilot only armed, flipped the override on, and took control back after touchdown. Horizontal hold **0.15–0.19 m rms**, roughly **1,600 gate fixes fused per run with none rejected by the filter**, no fence trip, no gate loss, no dropped log records.
- **The perception and estimation path runs real-time onboard.** Ported to C++ against the Python as a bit-identical reference — 4,975 detections over 602 frames, zero mismatching frames — the detector now runs **15 ms/frame median onboard** against the Python's 99 ms. Capture to fused fix measured **27 ms**, with **83% of frames fused**.
- **Course legs are flying.** Nine attempts in one evening reached three- to six-gate stop points, with vision steady at **24–34 accepted map fixes per second**, the yaw handoff stepping gate to gate in order on every flying run, and cross-track error held inside its corridor throughout.
- **The start pose is measured, not assumed.** Camera pitch solved from the pad against the first gate repeated to **±0.1°** across nine consecutive runs, and the start-pose check refused a takeoff that stood more than 2 m off its mark.

</Details>

<Details summary="What changes on hardware" hint="none of the simulator's guarantees survive">

The simulator handed over clean IMU samples, an ideal pinhole camera, and a race-status packet naming the active gate. A real drone gives none of that.

- **The IMU arrives from a commercial flight controller** over a serial link, carrying no sensor timestamps of its own — so samples are stamped on arrival rather than at capture.
- **The camera is a real camera.** A rolling shutter and a real lens, calibrated from a ChArUco video, with distortion now carried through every PnP and every closed-form back-projection — because `(u − cₓ)/fₓ` is only a ray for an ideal lens. Mount tilt and the lever arm from the CG are measured per airframe, and each drone's tilt is solved from its own flight past ranged gates.
- **There is no gate counter.** The client works out its own position on the course, matching each sighting to the nearest mapped opening under an uncertainty-aware test with a rule to skip ambiguous matches — a wrong association corrupts the estimate the next association depends on.
- **The map is a prior, not truth.** Opening centres are converted from the organiser's dimensioned coordinate PDF: a metre-scale estimate on a course nobody has surveyed as built.
- **The flight controller's own rate loops sit between the geometric law and the motors**, so the plant being inverted now has a controller inside it — and authority has to be handed from a human pilot's sticks to the autonomy in flight, and back again on demand.

</Details>

<Details summary="The course and the aircraft" hint="three airframes, two loops">

The organiser's course is flown twice around, with the drone starting on the ground between the last gate and the first. Two features drive the reference design: one gate is a **vertical double gate**, its upper opening flown in one direction and its lower opening flown back underneath after a vertical half-loop, and another is a **split-S**, overflown in one direction and crossed in the other. The path the controller tracks is a smooth spline through the opening centres, shaped through both of those loops so the reference stays flyable where it doubles back on itself.

Three airframes — Bilbo, Frodo and Gollum — each carry an onboard GPU compute module that runs vision, estimation, guidance, control and logging entirely onboard; a commercial flight controller for stabilization and motor output; and a camera with hardware capture timestamps. Every calibration — IMU, intrinsics, tilt, lever arm, latency, hover throttle — is per drone and version-controlled, alongside a mirror of each board's deployed tree so every aircraft keeps a history.

Onboard logging is a self-describing binary topic log with a single bounded asynchronous writer; any queue loss is recorded in the trailer rather than silently dropped. The flight controller's own blackbox log runs alongside it as the PID-rate authority for gyro, setpoint and motor data, tied to the companion log by a two-anchor clock mapping at arm and disarm.

</Details>

<Details summary="Bring-up discipline" hint="why the order matters">

Nothing gets built on an unverified subsystem. Compute and the flight-controller link first; then camera calibration; then flight-controller tuning and logged manual flights; then state estimation from the IMU alone, tuned on those logs; then RC-to-autonomy handover; then vision on top of the IMU-only estimator; and only then guidance and control, flown as slowly as the law allows.

Two reasons for that order. **Isolation** — when the integrated system misbehaves, a verified subsystem can be ruled out instead of suspected. **Baselines** — each step leaves a measured number the next one is judged against, and the IMU-only drift on logged manual flights is the figure vision has to beat. Logged flights replay offline, so estimator tuning costs no flight time.

One principle carries over from the simulator unchanged: **degrade rather than reject, and be honest about which axis is bad.** Keep the bearing and inflate the range. Demote a doubtful association rather than discard it. Carry an unexplained innovation as covariance instead of forcing it into the state.

</Details>
