AHAB (Aquatic High-endurance Aerial Biomonitor) is a solar-powered seaplane UAV designed for persistent marine mammal monitoring over remote ocean regions. The project spans conceptual vehicle design with a novel wing-rotation mechanism, an MDP-based flight planning framework that outperforms heuristic approaches, and a phased prototype flight test campaign.

---

<section id="motivation">

## The Problem

Cetacean populations are declining worldwide, and every existing monitoring platform gives something up:

- **Ship-based surveys** — expensive and weather-dependent.
- **Fixed-wing UAVs** (ScanEagle-class) — manual launch and recovery, limited range.
- **Surface vehicles** (SailDrone-class) — too slow for focal-animal tracking.
- **Passive acoustic buoys** — cannot be repositioned.

A solar-powered seaplane closes that gap on four fronts: persistent deployment via water landings and solar recharging, enough airspeed for focal monitoring, autonomous operation that removes ship-based recovery costs, and dual-modality sensing — visual survey while airborne, hydrophone acoustics while floating.

</section>

<section id="conceptual-design">

## Conceptual Design

The airframe has to satisfy aerodynamic performance, hydrostatic stability, and solar harvesting at once. A **flying hull** configuration won over floatplane and amphibious alternatives on structural weight and rough-sea takeoff performance.

- **Design point.** The full-payload vehicle is sized to a **5.0 kg** all-up design target, cruising on a **150 W at 18 m/s** power budget set by the VSPAERO model and validated across flight tests.
- **Design loop.** Geometry definition → mass estimation → parasitic drag in OpenVSP (**C_D₀ ≈ 0.031**) → endurance calculation → hydrostatic stability check, tuning beam, deadrise angle, and forebody/step/sternpost proportions until every requirement closes.
- **Floating roll stability.** The hull cross-section places the center of gravity above the center of buoyancy at the design waterline, giving a positive metacentric height so the hull self-rights across the expected heel range.
- **Wing-rotation mechanism.** A custom spanwise mechanism tilts the solar panels toward the sun while moored — and the same actuators and mechanical lock perform self-righting from inverted, with no added hardware. Both functions have since been demonstrated on the water (see Phase 3).
- **Measured payoff.** Over a simulated week in late June at 25°N, the articulated wing collects **+203 Wh** net over a fixed-wing baseline — a **21.9% energy gain** that widens the latitude and seasonal envelope for closing the daily energy budget.
- **Solar modeling.** pvlib with Ascent Solar CIGS thin-film parameters projects **1.3 flight-hours/day in January** and **2.3 in June** at the target latitude, clearing the requirement of at least one flight-hour per day year-round.

</section>

<section id="flight-planning">

## Optimal Flight Planning

The vehicle must continually decide when to fly, when to rest and recharge, and how to weigh observation time against battery exhaustion or loss. Weather is stochastic, so intuitive threshold rules ("fly above X, land below Y") leave real performance on the table.

- **Formulation.** Modeled as a **Markov Decision Process** solved by stochastic dynamic programming (Bellman recursion). State captures battery state-of-charge and operating mode — floating, flying, or failed; each stage selects takeoff, cruise, land, or save-energy.
- **Weather models.** Driven by stochastic models fit to ERA5 reanalysis — solar irradiance as a Beta-distributed clearness index, wind speed as a Weibull distribution — across five candidate deployment sites spanning **20°N to 58°N**.
- **Deployment.** The value function is computed offline and stored onboard for real-time decision-making.
- **Why it beats thresholds.** The optimal policy makes non-obvious calls a fixed threshold cannot reproduce — such as resting through a sunny period to bank energy for a risky overnight segment.
- **Monte Carlo results.** Across battery capacities, failure penalties, locations, and seasons, the optimal policy outperforms *every* threshold parameter combination — more whale sightings, lower failure rates — and adapts without location-specific tuning.
- **Design takeaway.** Energy-optimal battery capacity is **mission-location dependent**, so battery sizing should follow the intended operating environment.

<section id="prototype">

## Prototype: AHAB

AHAB is a proof-of-concept built to show the airframe can fly the missions the MDP framework plans, developed through a phased flight test campaign.

- **Construction.** 3D-printed in LW-ASA, reinforced with carbon-fiber spars and fiberglass, waterproofed with marine-grade sealant and potted electronics.
- **Changes from the conceptual design.** A V-tail for easier construction, and sponsons for improved hydrostatic stability.

<Figure src="/images/seaplane/ahab-afloat-2026-10-07.jpg" alt="AHAB floating level and upright on a lake, seen from the rear quarter with both propellers and wingtip floats visible" caption="AHAB afloat during the 7 October 2026 lake test — flying hull, V-tail, and wingtip floats." number={1} wide={true} />

### Phase 1: Aerodynamic Validation

Phase 1 confirmed the airframe is flightworthy — minimal trim corrections, significant excess thrust, and excess control authority in cruise.

- **All-up weight, as flown:** 3.75 kg — the Phase 1 prototype, against the 5.0 kg full-payload design target
- **Cruise power:** ~150 W at 18 m/s and 35% throttle
- **Glide ratio:** 6.5
- **Takeoff run:** 4–5 seconds

<Video src="/images/seaplane/Takeoff.mov" caption="Phase 1 maiden flight takeoff." number={2} />

### Phase 2: Hydrostatic & Hydrodynamic Validation

Phase 2 validates water operations. The second prototype is fully waterproofed and hydrostatically stable. Waterproofing tests, low-speed taxi runs, planing on the step and water takeoffs have all been flown, from the first water takeoff on 27 February 2026 through the October 2026 lake tests, with spray rails and wingtip floats redesigned along the way.

### Phase 3: Wing Rotation — Solar Tracking & Self-Righting

Phase 3 puts the wing-rotation mechanism to work on the water. Both of its functions have now been demonstrated on the floating vehicle:

- **Floating solar tracking.** The moored vehicle rotates its wing spanwise to aim the panels at the sun — the mechanism behind the **+203 Wh** weekly gain (**21.9%**) predicted in the conceptual design.
- **Self-righting.** The same actuators and mechanical lock recover the vehicle from an inverted attitude, with no additional hardware.
